# Neon Tap M6: the music follows the effort

Design doc 8's "Generated audio with the effort-driven music layers", added to
`programs/neontap.c` 2026-10-30.

## Premise

Design doc 8 asks for "a music loop built in layers. A bass layer plays at the
auto tier, a drum layer at the regular tier and a lead layer at tap speed.
The music literally follows your effort." The M6 milestone row is just
"Generated audio with the effort-driven music layers."

The machine has **one square-wave voice** (SPEC 6: `AUDIO_FREQ` + `AUDIO_CTRL`,
no mixing), so the three named layers cannot sound at once. The faithful
reading is therefore a *continuous loop whose character changes with the
effort tier*: tempo, note-pattern and root pitch are all selected from the
tier, so the music you hear is literally driven by how fast you tap.

## Mechanism

- `music_tier(s)` splits `tap_speed` (0..256, from `tc_speed`) into auto
  (<=63), regular (64..170) and tap (>170).
- `music_tick()` runs every play frame and advances a step counter against
  `MUS_STEP[tier] = {30, 15, 8}` frames/step → **2, 4 and 7.5 steps/s**. A
  change of tier restarts the bar (`mus_step = 0`), so a new layer enters on
  its downbeat.
- Each tier carries its own 8-note pattern `MUS_PAT[tier]` over its own root
  `MUS_ROOT = {55, 110, 220}` Hz — auto is a low sparse drone (bass), regular
  a mid driving climb (drums), tap a high staccato leap (lead). The pattern is
  applied as a power-of-two pitch ladder on one voice.
- `snd_frame()` arbitration: an active `snd()` effect owns the voice; when it
  ends it sets `mus_hold`, which holds one silent frame so an effect reads as
  a tick rather than a click, then `music_frame()` resumes. The charge whine
  still wins while it is sounding.

`music_tick` is called *before* the frame's note is due but *after* `tap_speed`
is updated (line 806), so the tempo tracks this frame's effort exactly.

## Observables

`mus_bar` — steps taken in the current bar (resets each bar, `0..7`); read by
the test through `--vars`. It is deliberately *not* drawn on the HUD, so the
music is audible-only.

## Measured

Three controlled probes (generated by `tests/test_neontap.sh` §3c), each
holding one tier for a whole run, measured over the frames the tier actually
held:

| probe | held tier | measured | design |
|---|---|---|---|
| idle (no tap) | auto | 1.95 steps/s | 2 |
| A every 10 frames | regular | 3.94 steps/s | 4 |
| A every 2 frames | tap | 7.51 steps/s | 7.5 |

Each probe is rejected if a *different* tier holds more than a fifth of the
frames it drove — so a cadence that lands in the wrong tier fails rather than
quietly measuring the wrong thing.

Shipped 4000-frame session: 3 tier changes total, 3908 frames in tier 2 at
7.51 steps/s — the bar-reset-on-tier-change costs ~0 in practice.

## Findings

1. **The layers must tick independently of the effects.** The first version
   only advanced the music when no effect was sounding; during combat the
   effects are dense, so the *tap* tier — the one that should sing loudest —
   starved to 2.69 steps/s while idle sat at its nominal rate. Splitting the
   clock (`music_tick`, always) from the voice (`music_frame`, when free)
   fixed it: the tempo now tracks effort even mid-combat, and effects still
   own the pitch.

2. **A doubling step count cannot express three distinct tempos.** The
   original `MUS_STEP` was `{30, 15, 7}` frames — but a 7-frame step is 8.57
   steps/s, not 8, and halving further drifts. A fixed table of
   `{30, 15, 8}` gives exactly 2/4/7.5 with an integer frame count, at the
   cost of the top tier being 7.5 rather than a round 8.

3. **M6 is audible-only.** The framebuffer is **byte-identical across all
   4000 frames** versus M5 — the music changed only machine state (the audio
   registers and the `mus_*` globals in RAM, which the state hash covers).
   So the golden relocks on `hash=` alone (4000/4000 frames) while `fb=`
   does not move. This is the cleanest possible split between "what you hear"
   and "what you see", and it is why the card's golden change is large
   (every frame hash) but behaviourally narrow (one subsystem, no pixels).

4. **The bar-reset on tier change is cheap in practice.** Resetting
   `mus_step` on every tier change would, in a session that oscillates, cut
   the measured tempo below nominal. The shipped session changes tier only 3
   times in 4000 frames, so the tap tier measures 7.51 against a nominal 7.5
   — the reset is a rounding error, not a tax. It is still the right thing:
   a new layer should enter on its downbeat.

## Consequences / not done

- The tempo ladder tops out at 7.5 steps/s, not 8, because 8 steps/s would
  need a 7.5-frame step. Fractional frames were rejected (an integer frame
  counter is deterministic and obvious); 7.5 reads as "fast enough".
- The three layers are sequential, never simultaneous — the machine has one
  voice. A future card with a second voice could sound bass and lead
  together; nothing here blocks that.
- No music on the title or game-over screens (`state != 1` keeps the voice
  silent), by choice: the design ties the loop to effort, and there is no
  effort off the field.
- `mus_bar` is a test hook, not a game mechanic — it is not drawn and does
  not affect scoring.
